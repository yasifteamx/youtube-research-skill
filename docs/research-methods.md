# Research methods — how to rank a topic, spot a niche, audit a competitor

The method behind the `youtube-research-skill`. Tools change; the method is
what keeps you from building a video nobody watches.

## Rank a viral topic

Rank by **views velocity** (views in the first days), not lifetime views. A
video doing 100K in 3 days beats a 2M video from 2021.

1. List candidate videos with: title, views, age, channel size, format.
2. Compute velocity: views ÷ days since publish. Note the outliers.
3. For the top 2–3, write down the formula, not just the topic: the exact
   title shape ("___ Has No Idea ___"), the reveal structure (when the twist
   lands), the first-30-seconds promise, the thumbnail pairing.
4. Ask: is the topic fresh or a re-run? A fresh case or a genuine twist wins;
   a compilation of old news with no new angle loses. Check demand signals
   (search trend direction, not just volume) before committing.
5. Record the source for every factual claim — outlet + date. If a claim
   traces to a single thin source, flag it. If the only source is a site
   known to fabricate "solved" stories, drop the topic and say why.

## Spot a niche or pattern

A pattern is worth more than a topic list. Look for:

- **A title formula that repeats across winners** — same shape, different
  cases. Note the year counts, the reveal verb, the time gap.
- **A sub-pattern inside a niche** — e.g. suspects arrested decades later,
  elderly suspects, a specific forensic method (DNA, genealogy) as the
  reveal engine.
- **A format gap** — something adjacent channels are not doing: an on-camera
  host, a forensic explainer series, regional cases in English, a
  Shorts-to-long funnel, live premieres with polls.
- **A demand gap** — a creator who stopped posting in a format viewers still
  ask for in comments. Comments asking "where is the next one?" are a brief.

Log every find with: what it is, why it could work, what it would need, and
one concrete next video it implies. A find with no implied video is a note,
not a lead.

## Audit a competitor

1. Upload cadence (videos/month) and view distribution: which 20% of videos
   drive 80% of views? Those are the channel's real niche — ignore the rest.
2. Title/thumbnail patterns on the top 10: count the words, note the banner,
   the face, the colour. Check the package at 120px.
3. Format: runtime band, hook structure (first 30 seconds), cut pace
   (cuts per minute), real footage vs stills, music, subtitles.
4. Comment sentiment: what do viewers praise, what do they keep asking for,
   what do they complain about? The complaints are your edge.
5. What they are NOT doing: the gap you can own without copying them.

## Package before publish

- Title + thumbnail are one pairing — lint them together, never separately.
- Title under 50 chars, hook front-loaded in the first 45 characters.
- Thumbnail ≤5 words, never duplicating the title. Check it at 120px.
- Packaging is locked a day before publish, not after.

## First 48 hours

- Community teaser + Short 24h before publish.
- Reply to every comment in the first 2 hours; pin a question comment.
- CTR check at hour 4–6 — swap the thumbnail only if CTR < ~3%.
- Review the trajectory at hour 48. Don't judge a video in its first 7 days.
- Read retention to the exact second people left; that second is the next
  video's fix.
